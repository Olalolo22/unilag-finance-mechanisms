## 1. The Robinson Crusoe Economy (No Capital Markets)
* **The Setting:** A one-person, one-good economy with no uncertainty.
* **The Problem:** The decision-maker (Robinson Crusoe) must choose between consumption now (`C_0`) and consumption in the future (`C_1`).
* **The Constraint:** The decision not to consume now is identically the decision to invest. Production and consumption decisions are strictly coupled.

### Two Necessary Sets of Information:
1. **Subjective Trade-Offs (Preferences):** 
   * Represented by **Indifference Curves** (Utility curves `U_1`, `U_2`).
   * **Marginal Rate of Substitution (MRS):** The slope of the indifference curve:
     `MRS = dC_1 / dC_0 = -(1 + r)`
     * Also called the *subjective rate of time preference*.
     * The indifference curve is convex to the origin: as consumption today decreases, an individual demands relatively more future consumption to maintain equal total utility.
2. **Productive Opportunity Set (Technology):**
   * Shows feasible trade-offs between present and future consumption technologically possible.
   * **Marginal Rate of Transformation (MRT):** The slope of the production opportunity curve:
     * Represents the rate at which one Naira of consumption foregone today is transformed into future consumption.
     * Decreasing slope reflects the law of **diminishing marginal returns to investment**.

### Optimal Equilibrium Without Capital Markets:
* The individual invests until the subjective rate of time preference equals the marginal return on productive investment:
  `MRS = MRT`
* The individual cannot reach a higher utility curve than their own physical production set allows.

## 2. Introducing Capital Markets (The Fisher Separation Theorem)

### A. The Role of Capital Markets
* Capital markets introduce the ability to borrow or lend at a market-determined interest rate `r`.
* **Capital Market Line (CML):**
  * Intercept on future consumption axis: `W_1`
  * Slope: `-(1 + r)`
  * Equation: `C_1 = W_1 - (1 + r) * C_0`
* **Intertemporal Exchange:**
  * Money lent today (`X_0`) returns principal plus interest: `X_1 = X_0 * (1 + r)`
  * Movement along the capital market line does not change total wealth, but allows the individual to shift consumption across time to reach a higher utility curve (`U_3 > U_2`).

### B. The Fisher Separation Theorem (Formal Statement)
> *"Given perfect and complete capital markets, the firm's production/investment decision is governed solely by an objective market criterion (maximizing attained wealth, NPV > 0) without regard to the subjective consumption preferences of individual shareholders."*

### The Two Separate Steps:
1. **Step 1 (Production Decision):** Invest in physical projects until the marginal rate of return on investment equals the market interest rate (`MRT = 1 + r`). This maximizes current wealth `W_0*`.
2. **Step 2 (Consumption Decision):** Borrow or lend along the capital market line until the subjective time preference equals the market rate (`MRS = 1 + r`).

### Key Exam Implications:
1. **Shareholder Unanimity:** All shareholders—regardless of whether they are impatient (prefer cash now) or patient (prefer future growth)—agree that management should simply maximize the market value of the firm (NPV).
2. **Delegation of Management:** Ownership can be separated from control. Shareholders can delegate capital budgeting to professional managers without managers needing to know the personal utility curves of individual investors.
3. **Breakdown of Separation (Transaction Costs & Imperfect Markets):**
   * When borrowing rate `r_b` exceeds lending rate `r_l` (transaction costs / bid-ask spreads), the single capital market line breaks into a kinked line.
   * Shareholders can no longer agree on a single discount rate, and separation breaks down.
